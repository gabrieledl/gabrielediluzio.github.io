About

Researching dependence in financial markets.

I develop econometric methods for measuring dynamic, nonlinear and tail dependence across financial assets.

Research profile

My work combines time-series econometrics, copula models and high-dimensional dependence estimation. A central part of my research examines how climate-related information affects credit-market dependence and systemic risk.

Selected research

Current projects.

Research projects in financial dependence, climate risk and high-dimensional covariance modelling.

01

Modelling the Impact of Climate News on CDS Dependence

A dynamic factor copula framework with score-driven loadings for studying how physical and transition climate risks affect dependence across European credit default swaps.

Working paper
02

Dynamic Conditional Dependence and Covariance Estimation

High-dimensional covariance estimation combining volatility dynamics with robust rank-based measures of dependence.

Doctoral research
03

Tail Dependence and Systemic Credit Risk

Measurement of joint distress probabilities and tail dependence across firms, industries and periods of financial or climate-related stress.

Research project
04

Systemic Illiquidity and Flexible Copula Models

A framework for integrating realized illiquidity, latent systemic factors and nonlinear dependence into systemic-risk measurement.
